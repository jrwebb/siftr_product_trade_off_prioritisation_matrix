Why Siftr

Commercial rigour that protects customer trust

Most prioritisation frameworks fail because the often biased scoring efforts reward whoever lobbies loudest. Siftr anchors every bet in real economics, pairing concrete subscriber volume and CLTV cohorts with evidence confidence, reversibility, and counter-metric guardrails, so teams agree not just on what to build next, but what to test first and what to deliberately leave behind.

How Siftr scores and de-risks product bets

Instead of arbitrary 1–10 sliders, Siftr anchors every initiative in concrete customer volume, subscriber lifetime value (CLTV), and evidence confidence—then checks whether the bet can be reversed or needs a small validation step first.

1. Siftr Composite Score

Combines Acquisition Volume, Engagement & Retention, Strategic Fit, and Annualised CLTV (£)—multiplied by Evidence Confidence (0.5× to 1.0×), divided by Delivery Effort (XS–XL), and adjusted for Reversibility and Risk.

2. Reversibility (Doors)

Type 2 (Two-Way Door): Easy to roll back via a feature flag or A/B test. Type 1 (One-Way Door): Pricing changes, billing migrations, or public commitments that are costly to undo and require higher evidence first.

3. Counter-Metric Guardrail

The vital customer or commercial metric that must not degrade while chasing your primary goal. Examples: lifting trial starts without hurting 30-day renewal, adding alerts without raising opt-outs, or speeding up pages without cutting ad yield.

4. Validation / Thin-Slice Step

The smallest, cheapest experiment to test your riskiest assumption before committing full engineering capacity. Examples: a fake-door button test, interactive Figma prototype, manual concierge trial, or 10% traffic holdout.
